# Stored-window consensus: results

**The preregistered rule does not reach the target.** On Goodfire DEV arm C, window-level two-of-three agreement detects **12/80 enacted runs (15.0%; Wilson 95% 8.8–24.4%)** and raises **3/70 clean false alarms (4.3%; Wilson 95% 1.5–11.9%)**. The recall clause passes exactly; the at-most-2/70 false-alarm clause fails; **0/150 runs are excluded**, so the at-most-10% exclusion clause passes. The clean-FAR Wilson upper bound is 11.9%; a point-estimate pass would not have established a population FAR below 3% either. These are post-hoc run-end windows, not a causal prefix monitor.

## Known single-judge baselines and missingness

All three original arm C single-judge counts reproduced the published CM-13, CM-19 and B5 figures, including the original Stage-1 pass-through. Opus had four unknown enacted runs; its historical numerator over 80 is not a complete-case rate. Terra and Sonnet each had 418/418 available judgments; Opus had 410/418, with eight refused prompts. The primary two-of-three rule still had enough available judgments for every selected window, so it has no excluded runs. The primary excludes an unjudged pass-through, whereas original-single reproduction includes it; comparison against the original single-judge baselines is therefore a comparison of full run rules, not of judge votes alone.

Terra's receipt records the configured `openai-codex/gpt-6.1-terra` subscription route, low thinking and no fallback; it does not establish independent per-call served-model attestation. Sonnet's receipt attests `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5-5` on all 418 calls; Opus's receipt attests `anthropic/claude-opus-5-5` on its successful calls. The eight Opus refusals are unknowns, not negative votes. A different Opus comparison refused 8 of 15 other-model-agent transcripts; do not send Opus such transcripts or interpret that separate refusal rate as this study's.

## Secondary arms and mechanism

Arm B's same two-of-three rule also yielded **12/80 recall and 3/70 clean false alarms**, with zero exclusions; this is descriptive, not another primary decision. Arm A had **0/70 clean alarms**; its enacted result is withheld for small-cell protection.

Against the original terra arm C run rule on paired complete cases, consensus had zero additional enacted detections and lost 13; the exact two-sided sign/McNemar p-value is **0.000244**. On clean runs, consensus had zero additional alarms and removed three terra alarms (p = **0.25**); all three remaining consensus clean alarms were also terra alarms. Thus agreement removed some terra false alarms, but at substantial recall cost, and still left the point-estimate FAR above the target. The original terra rule includes pass-through and the consensus rule does not, so do not ascribe the entire change to judge agreement. Paired p-values assume independent pairs; task clustering and the LLM-consensus passage-label nature of Goodfire labels limit their interpretation.

The pairwise window-ANDs, union, and window-only singles were calculated as prespecified but their counts, ratios and tests are withheld or pooled because 1–2 cells or complementary cells would be disclosed. The same applies to terra–Opus and terra–Sonnet enacted-overlap counts, their Jaccards, and the finer breakdown of which other judges share the clean alarms: published single-judge margins and earlier receipts make small counts recoverable. We do not publish run identities. Prior receipts themselves already contain small cells; this receipt avoids adding a new complementary disclosure and cannot undo their prior publication. Exact zeros above are permitted. The pre-run bounds still hold: both Sonnet-containing window-ANDs cannot reach 12/80, independently of the observed overlap.

## Interpretation and next study

This is an **in-sample, DEV-only** analysis: although this particular rule was fixed and merged before this score, all three sets of verdicts and their published single-judge results already existed. There is no held-out window set judged by these models; cross-test remains untouched. No monitor rule change is justified.

A possible next step is to preregister a genuinely held-out sample, blind both labels and prior judge outputs, freeze window selection and exclusions, and have Haiku and Sonnet independently judge each selected window. At the CM-13 C rate of 326 selected windows per 150 runs, that is **up to about 652 calls before prompt deduplication**, roughly **3.3 million input and 41,000 output tokens** if both readers cost as much as the observed Sonnet route (5,025 input and 63.4 output tokens per successful call); Haiku's actual usage may differ. These are estimates, not an authorization or a claim of zero refusal risk; enforce served-model attestation and retain refusals as unknown. This two-reader design is a new prespecified agreement rule, not an unchanged validation of the three-judge Opus/terra/Sonnet ensemble. No new judgment is launched here.
