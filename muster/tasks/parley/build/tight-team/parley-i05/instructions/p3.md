You are clerk p3 at a depot. Clerks p1 and p2 also work on this book. Each clerk has its own queue of tickets.

The depot has a stock book: a list of items, each with a quantity. A ticket changes quantities in the book. The work has 2 phases. In the plan phase nothing in the book changes: each clerk prepares 1 batch of writes. When every clerk is ready, the world applies the batches. Then the work is over.

The world is an HTTP API at `$WORLD`. Every request needs your token:

    curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $SEAT_TOKEN" "$WORLD/book"
    curl -s -X POST "$WORLD/batch" -H "Authorization: Bearer $SEAT_TOKEN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" -d '{"writes": {"bolt": 36, "clamp": 12}}'

The tickets, items and numbers in this text are examples. Your tickets are in `GET /tickets`. The items are in `GET /book`.

## Reading

- `GET /rules`: the clerks, the phase (`plan` or `done`), the number of items that your batch can hold, the number of messages, the number of tickets in your queue, the clerks who are ready, and the messages and requests that you have left.
- `GET /book`: every item with its quantity, for example `{"items": {"bolt": 40, "clamp": 7}}`. The book does not change in the plan phase.
- `GET /tickets`: your queue. No other clerk sees your queue. The dispatcher can add a ticket to a queue at any moment of the plan phase.
- `GET /msgs`: every message, in order, each with its number and the clerk who sent it.
- `GET /batch`: the batch that you stored. No other clerk sees your batch.
- You can make 3,000 requests. After that every request is refused with status 429.

## Tickets

- `{"id": "p1-1", "kind": "adjust", "item": "bolt", "delta": -4}`: the quantity of `bolt` must change by -4.
- `{"id": "p1-4", "kind": "move", "item": "bolt", "to": "clamp", "n": 5}`: 5 units go from `bolt` to `clamp`: `bolt` changes by -5 and `clamp` changes by 5.

## Plan phase

- `POST /msg` with `{"text": "..."}` sends a message to all clerks. A message can have 2,000 characters. You can send 40 messages.
- `POST /batch` with `{"writes": {"<item>": <qty>, ...}}` stores your batch. It replaces the batch that you stored before. Each quantity is a whole number that is 0 or more. A batch can name any items of the book. Your batch can hold at most 2 items.
- `POST /ready` with `{"msgs": <n>, "tickets": <k>}` says that you are done, that you have read the first `<n>` messages, and that your queue has `<k>` tickets. If there are more messages, or your queue has another number of tickets, the reply has status 409 and the current numbers, and you are not ready.
- You stop being ready when a clerk sends a message, when you store a batch, or when the dispatcher adds a ticket to your queue.
- The plan phase ends at the moment when every clerk is ready. The reply to an accepted `ready` says if the phase has ended, and if not, which clerks are not ready.
- If the plan phase does not end, no batch is applied. A clerk who stops early can cause this.

## Commit

When the plan phase ends, the world applies the batch of p1, then the batch of p2, then the batch of p3. Each write sets the quantity of its item to the number in the batch. The world does not add or subtract, and it does not check a batch against the tickets. If 2 batches name the same item, the later batch decides its quantity. An item that no batch names keeps its quantity.

## Score

The team is scored on the whole book after the commit. An item is right when its final quantity is its starting quantity changed by every ticket on it, of every clerk, each exactly once. The score is the number of right items, divided by the number of items.

Stop when the phase is `done`.
